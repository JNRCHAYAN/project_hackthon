# Responsible Design Note

This prototype is a decision-support tool. It is not a surveillance system, not a
risk-scoring engine, and not an enforcement mechanism.

> **An anomaly is not proof of fraud.** No output of this platform determines
> wrongdoing, accuses any person, blocks any transaction, or moves any money.

## Privacy

- **No real data of any kind.** Every balance, transaction, outlet, and account
  identifier is generated by a seeded simulator. No production API, customer
  record, or provider system is contacted.
- **No real identity is representable.** Accounts are pseudonymous hashes
  (`h0123`) seeded within a run. There is no field for a name, phone number,
  account number, or national ID, so none can be stored even by accident.
- **No credentials, ever.** The platform never requests, stores, or transmits
  PINs, OTPs, passwords, private keys, or authentication data. There is no login
  and no user account. The only secret it reads is an optional `ANTHROPIC_AUTH_TOKEN`
  for narration phrasing, supplied by the operator's environment and never
  committed.
- **Small aggregates are not exposed.** The relationship view only surfaces
  accounts appearing at three or more outlets, and it says plainly that this is
  usually an ordinary customer moving through a market. It is a lead for a human,
  not a profile.

## Human review boundary

Risk signals are advisory. The escalation path is agent → field officer → area
manager → central operations → risk analyst, and **every judgement that matters is
made by a person.**

The platform never:

- makes or records a final fraud determination;
- automatically blocks, freezes, reverses, or recovers anything;
- accuses, disciplines, or names any agent, customer, or outlet as a wrongdoer;
- moves value, refills a wallet, or settles between providers.

The complete set of actions a case can take is:

```python
VALID_ACTIONS = {"acknowledge", "escalate", "resolve", "note"}
```

There is no financial verb in that set, and no function anywhere in the codebase
that transfers, converts, or refills value. The strongest thing the platform can do
is *recommend that a human arrange balance through the provider's own approved
channel*. A test asserts that no banned financial verb ever enters `VALID_ACTIONS`.

## False positives

This is the central ethical risk of anomaly detection, and it is treated as a
first-class engineering problem rather than a disclaimer.

- **An operational demand spike is expected to be flagged as such, not as
  suspicion.** The distinction between "this is Eid demand" and "this needs a
  human" is the product's main analytical claim. `context.py` classifies the same
  chart into demand-spike, data-quality problem, or requires-review, and states
  which hypotheses it *rejected* and why.
- **A balance-chain mismatch is never framed as suspicious activity.** It is
  classified as a data-quality problem, with the explicit rationale that a
  reconciliation failure is an integrity fault and does not evidence customer
  behaviour. An unexplained balance difference must not become an accusation
  against a customer.
- **Concentration is judged within the outlet's own baseline.** Detection is
  per-agent against that agent's own history, never cross-agent comparison. A
  low-volume outlet must not be permanently flagged for being small — a fairness
  requirement, not a tuning preference.
- **A control case guards the detector.** The simulator plants a legitimate
  Eid-window surge (Scenario B2) specifically to be *not* flagged. If the detector
  flags it, the harness reports a false positive. See
  [`DATA_AND_SIMULATION.md`](DATA_AND_SIMULATION.md) and
  [`METRICS.md`](METRICS.md).
- **Alert floors exist to protect analyst attention.** An alert is an assertion
  that somebody should act, so it must clear a confidence floor (0.50) and a
  minimum sample size (5 points). A steep slope fitted through three noisy points
  is not a finding, and flooding the queue with those would cost an analyst the
  real ones. Suppressed projections are still *shown* on the outlet card — they
  simply do not raise an alert.
- **Language is reviewed by lint, not by good intentions.** `narrative.lint()`
  rejects `fraud`, `fraudulent`, `cheating`, `criminal`, `প্রতারণা`, and `অপরাধ`
  in any generated text, and the same check runs against model output before it can
  be displayed.

## Uncertainty and degraded data

A degraded feed must never silently produce a confident conclusion. Feed health
propagates into every downstream confidence figure. Every row below is reachable
in the shipped demo world, and the last column names where each is planted — so a
reviewer can go and look at it rather than take the table's word for it:

| Feed status | Confidence multiplier | Condition | Planted at (12-outlet demo, seed 42) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `fresh` | 1.00 | feed updated within 5 minutes | every other position — 32 of the 36 |
| `delayed` | 0.80 | last update 5–30 minutes old | `AG-1005` / rocket, 17.5 min |
| `stale` | 0.50 | last update more than 30 minutes old | `AG-1004` / nagad, 45 min |
| `conflicting` | 0.30 | declared balance disagrees with the reconciled chain | `AG-1003` / bKash (the Scenario C drift) |
| `missing` | 0.00 | no feed at all | `AG-1006` / bKash (timestamp absent) |

Each degraded feed sits on its own outlet, which leaves that outlet's other two
providers as honest evidence to read it against — one bad feed does not make the
whole outlet unreadable. A test asserts that one ordinary world reaches all five
states and that replaying the classifier over the planted data reproduces each
one, so the table above cannot drift from the code.

**Withdrawal rule.** When a feed is `conflicting` or `missing`, the liquidity
projection is **suppressed entirely**. No recommendation is offered, because
offering one would be inventing confidence the data does not support. What the
user gets instead is a full four-part alert that says so — today, for the
scenario this demo ships with:

> **Situation** — Liquidity projection for bKash is unavailable: the feed cannot
> be trusted at this time.
> **Uncertainty** — The data is not reliable, so no projection and no number is
> offered — a guess here would be worse than no answer.
> **Next steps** — Verify the feed through the approved channel · Do not act on
> any projection until it is verified · Notify a senior officer if verification
> does not complete.

The same alert is assembled independently in Bengali, and it carries the
provider's last declared balance as evidence. The short policy statement the
suite asserts against — *"Provider feed data is unreliable. Verify the feed
before acting on any projection for this provider."* — lives in
`app/quality.py`; the rendered narrative lives in `app/narrative.py`.

Every projection is reported as an **interval**, never a bare point estimate —
*"exhausted in ~3.1 h (likely 2.4–4.0 h), confidence 0.71"* — and the interval is
derived from the residual spread of the fit, not guessed. A balance that is not
falling reports "no depletion projected" rather than a number, and an
already-exhausted balance reports zero hours remaining rather than a negative
duration.

## Provider boundaries

Each provider keeps its own operations process, rules, and data boundary. A
combined outlet view is permitted; one provider acting on another's balance is not.

- Cross-provider case actions are rejected in code and return **HTTP 403** with an
  explanatory boundary notice. This is demonstrated live rather than merely
  described.
- There is **no** conversion, settlement, or interoperation between provider
  balances anywhere in the system, and no code path that could perform one.
- When the dashboard suggests a remedy, it points to an **approved-channel balance
  arrangement** at the same provider — possibly coordinated with a nearby outlet
  holding headroom in that same provider. It never implies that one provider's
  balance can be used to cover another's.
- The unified view is read-only aggregation for the agent's benefit. It does not
  merge wallets, and it never presents a single blended total as the headline
  without the per-provider composition beside it.

The guardrail is generative rather than merely restrictive: because conversion is
prohibited, the only correct advice *is* an approved-channel arrangement made
before the projected exhaustion time. The recommendation follows from the
constraint.

## Actions the prototype intentionally does not perform

- Real interoperability, settlement, or conversion between bKash, Nagad, Rocket,
  or any other wallet.
- Access to production APIs, real customer identities, real balances, or real
  transaction accounts.
- Automatic blocking, accusation, disciplinary action, or final fraud
  determination.
- Unauthorized cash movement, wallet refill, transfer, recovery, or reversal.
- Collection of credentials, PINs, OTPs, passwords, or private authentication data.
- Any claim of regulatory approval or production fraud-detection readiness.

## Regulatory position

This is a **prototype built for a hackathon**, using synthetic data only. It has
not been reviewed or approved by any regulator, and it is not connected to any
financial infrastructure. Nothing here should be read as a claim of compliance
with any regulatory framework, nor as evidence that the approach is ready for
production fraud detection.

Its purpose is to demonstrate that multi-provider liquidity pressure, unusual
activity, and operational coordination can be surfaced together — explainably,
honestly about uncertainty, and safely, with a human making every decision that
matters.
