The problem

One drawer. Three balances. One question nobody can answer.

An agent shop in a busy market, the afternoon before Eid. It serves bKash, Nagad and Rocket customers from a single physical cash drawer — but each provider keeps its own e-money balance, on its own system.

Cash-out requests suddenly increase. The agent can inspect each provider separately, and can count the cash box. What the agent cannot do is add those things up into an answer, because they are not the same kind of thing.

Then it gets harder. Most of the pressure is coming from one provider. Several transactions are almost the same amount, from a small group of accounts. That could be ordinary Eid demand, a data problem, or activity a person needs to review — and the shop has no way to tell which.

And when it does go wrong, the agent, the field officer, the provider operations team and the risk reviewer may not know who should act first, or how the issue is followed until it is resolved.

What the agent sees today

bKash

Own screen. Own balance. Own feed.

Nagad

Own screen. Own balance. Own feed.

Rocket

Own screen. Own balance. Own feed.

Shared cash drawer

One physical balance, drained by every provider's cash-out demand. Not visible on any provider screen.

The missing question

"Will this shop have enough cash and enough of the right provider's e-money to keep serving customers for the next few hours?"

Three separate screens and a cash box do not add up to one operational answer. That gap is the product.

Users and roles

Five stakeholders, five different questions

A combined outlet view is permitted. One provider controlling another provider's balance, data or decisions is not. Every screen is built around that line.

Multi-provider agent

Sees physical cash and each provider balance together, understands upcoming pressure, and knows what action may be needed — including when to do nothing.

Provider operations / network coordination

Monitors assigned agents, reviews important alerts, contacts the correct agent or field officer, coordinates approved support, and owns the case until closure.

Risk or compliance analyst

Reviews unusual activity using evidence and context. Operations may escalate a case upward; the platform records review outcomes only, never a conclusion about a person.

Financial service provider

Understands provider-specific service pressure while provider data, authority and process stay separate. Provider boundaries are enforced in code.

Management

Sees area-level service risk, recurring problems and overall operational readiness — the hotspot ranking, not individual customer records.

Customers

The point of all of it: more reliable service from the provider they actually chose. No customer data exists in this prototype.

The escalation path modelled by the platform runs agent/outlet → field officer → area manager → central operations → risk analyst. Operations never makes a risk judgement; the analyst never sees a raw dashboard without evidence attached.

The core insight

Cash and e-money move in opposite directions

Customer actionShared cashThat provider's e-money
Cash-out takes cash, sends e-money ↓ decreases ↑ increases
Cash-in gives cash, takes e-money ↑ increases ↓ decreases

Encoded in the simulator as Transaction.cash_delta() and Transaction.emoney_delta() — the sign convention the whole product rests on, not a presentation choice.

1 · Aggregate health is misleading. Total value can look comfortable while one provider is hours from exhaustion. A single summed number is an actively dangerous metric, not a simplification. The platform therefore never shows only a total — the per-provider composition sits beside it, always.

2 · Cross-provider imbalance is structural, not a bug. Cash that accumulated from one provider's cash-out cannot be used to replenish another provider's e-money. That is settlement, and it is out of scope — so the platform does not offer it, anywhere, at any confidence level.

The guardrail generates the recommendation. Because conversion is impossible, the only correct remedy is an approved-channel balance arrangement, before the projected exhaustion time. The advice follows from the constraint. That is the product.

Layering rule

Analytics modules are pure functions over plain dataclasses — no network, no database, no globals, and nothing in the core imports the engine. Enforced by the import graph, not by convention. This is why the validation harness and the what-if re-run are nearly free.

One consistent snapshot

/api/state returns the whole dashboard in one request. The snapshot is internally consistent by construction; parallel calls could render a torn view where the headline total and the outlet list disagree.

Monitoring and observability

/healthz liveness (also the cold-start warm-up target) · /api/metrics precision, recall and false-positive rate against ground truth · offline harness python -m app.validation over five fixed seeds · every alert carries evidence[], uncertainty, rejected_hypotheses[], recommended_steps[].

Liquidity intelligence

An interval, honestly earned — never a point estimate

How the number is produced

A Theil–Sen robust slope over a trailing 60-minute window, fitted separately for shared cash and for each provider's e-money. A robust estimator is required because a naive mean is destroyed by a single large transaction — precisely the Eid-rush situation the product must survive.

  • Interval from the fit, not from a guess. Bounds come from the residual quantiles of the fitted slope (p10/p90), so the band widens where the data is noisy.
  • Confidence is a product, not a vibe. Feed freshness × rate variance × sample size — it degrades honestly instead of asserting false precision.
  • Not falling means no number. A stable or rising balance reports "no depletion projected" rather than a figure.
  • Already empty reports zero hours remaining, never a negative duration.

Honest-uncertainty rules, in the product

Every projection is rendered as "exhausted in ~3.1 h (likely 2.4–4.0 h), confidence 0.71" — the shape matters as much as the number. Two floors protect analyst attention: an alert needs confidence ≥ 0.50 and at least 5 data points. Suppressed projections are still shown on the outlet card; they simply raise no alert, because a steep slope fitted through three noisy points is not a finding and flooding the queue with those would cost an analyst the real ones.

Withdrawal rule — a failure mode, not a fallback guess

When a feed is conflicting or missing, the liquidity projection is suppressed entirely and replaced with a verification notice: "Provider feed data is unreliable. Verify the feed before acting on any projection for this provider." No recommendation is offered, because offering one would be inventing confidence the data does not support. This path is asserted by tests, not merely documented.

Unusual activity, classified — the differentiator

The same chart can mean three different things. The alert says which, and why.

Operational demand spike

Velocity up, and aligned to known context (Eid window, salary days 1–5, market day), and spread across ≥8 diverse accounts, and a broad amount distribution.

→ Review priority is suppressed and the benign hypothesis is explained to the user. This is the outcome that keeps the queue trustworthy.

Requires review

Velocity up, and concentrated in ≤5 accounts, and amounts near-identical (±2%), and no context alignment.

→ Raises an alert with evidence and uncertainty, routed to a person. Advisory language only.

Data-quality problem

The balance chain fails to reconcile — declared balance disagrees with the replay of opening balance plus signed transactions.

→ Routed as data_quality and framed as an integrity fault, never as evidence about customer behaviour. An unexplained balance difference must not become a claim against anyone.

Every alert names the hypotheses it rejected — and why. Detection emits a classification with reasoning, not a bare score. A "why flagged" panel lists the explanations that were considered and set aside. That single panel is what lets a reviewer argue with the system instead of having to trust it.

What the shipped Bengali alert actually says

সব তথ্য এখনো নিশ্চিত নয় — এটি সাধারণ চাহিদাও হতে পারে। তাই কোনো সিদ্ধান্তের আগে একজন কর্মকর্তার পর্যালোচনা প্রয়োজন।

Assembled by narrative.py — situation, uncertainty, then next steps, in Bengali and English. Numbers are rendered directly from the analytics layer and never pass through the language model, so the model is structurally incapable of displaying a wrong figure.

Two detection families, both grounded in the brief

  • Burst velocity with near-identical amounts — count of transactions in a trailing 12-minute window whose amounts fall within ±2% of one another, from a small set of distinct pseudonymous accounts (ceiling 5).
  • Balance-chain reconciliation failure — balance_after does not reconcile against opening balance plus the sum of signed transactions (tolerance 1%).

Fairness is a design requirement here

Scoring is per-outlet against that outlet's own history, never cross-outlet comparison. A low-volume outlet must not be permanently flagged merely for being small. The cost of that choice is stated plainly on the limitations slide.

The control case is the point

The simulator plants a legitimate Eid-window surge (14 diverse-account cash-outs, broad amount range) specifically to be not flagged. A detector that flags it as well as the injected pattern — or that cannot tell them apart — is measuring nothing, and the harness reports it as a false positive.

An unusual pattern is not evidence of wrongdoing. It is a reason for a person to look. Nothing here scores, ranks, names or accuses anyone.

Append-only audit trail

Every transition — ownership, acknowledgement, escalation, note, resolution — is appended to SQLite with actor, action and timestamp. The dashboard's case panel and /api/cases read from it, and /api/export packages alerts, thresholds, projections, feed status and case history into one downloadable evidence pack, so a reviewer never has to take the dashboard's word for anything.

The complete set of actions a case can take

VALID_ACTIONS = {"acknowledge", "escalate",
                 "resolve", "note"}

There is no financial verb in that set. The strongest thing the platform can do is recommend that a human arrange balance through the provider's own approved channel. A test asserts that no banned financial operation verb ever enters this set.

Stated honestly: on the free-tier deployment the audit log lives on ephemeral disk, so a case history does not survive a restart or redeploy. See limitations.

Provider boundaries

Refused in code, not discouraged in copy

Enforced on every case transition

coordination.provider_boundary_ok() is consulted before any action is applied. An action whose acting provider differs from the alert's provider raises PermissionError, which the API converts to HTTP 403 carrying a boundary notice — the dashboard renders it as an explanation, not a generic error. The demo attempts a cross-provider action on purpose and shows the refusal.

Shared physical cash is the one deliberate exception: an alert with no provider tag belongs to no track, so any track may coordinate on it.

There is no function to call

No code path converts, transfers, settles or refills value between providers. The forbidden financial verbs are kept as a named constant precisely so the guardrail is data that can be asserted against:

FORBIDDEN_ACTION_VERBS = ("transfer", "refill",
                          "block", "freeze", "reverse")
  • The unified view is read-only aggregation for the agent. It does not merge wallets, and never presents a blended total as the headline without the per-provider composition beside it.
  • Escalation stays inside the provider's own track. A risk analyst receives escalations, but the platform records review outcomes only.

The guardrail generates the recommendation. This is the part that makes the constraint productive rather than merely restrictive.

The tempting (wrong) moveWhat the platform says instead
Move value from a provider holding surplus into the provider running dry Refused. That is settlement, and it is out of scope. No such action exists in the code.
Blend all three balances into one headline and call the outlet healthy Refused. Composition sits beside the total, always, because the total can look fine while one provider is hours from empty.
— Instead: recommend an approved-channel balance arrangement at the same provider, before the projected exhaustion time — possibly coordinated with a nearby outlet holding headroom in that same provider, discovered by distance.

Exactly one provider's e-money can be at risk while the aggregate looks fine. Surfacing that — without ever implying one provider may act on another's balance — is the whole trick.

Five metrics, measured against planted ground truth — not asserted

Produced by python -m app.validation over seeds 1–5 at 8 outlets × 3 providers, written to data/metrics.md and data/metrics.json. Every figure below comes from the harness; none is a claim. Narration ran with the LLM disabled, so these numbers involve no network call.

1 · Shortage detection lead time

14.76 min

Advance warning at detection, mean and median. 5 of 5 planted shortages detected, 0 missed. Predicted horizon landed within 0.24 min of the planted drain rate.

2 · Anomaly precision / recall

1.00 / 1.00

tp 10 · fp 0 · fn 0 against the labelled injected episodes — 5 via the anomaly detector, 5 via data-quality reconciliation. Precision answers "when we flag, how often are we right"; recall answers "of the cases genuinely planted, how many did we catch".

3 · False positives on legitimate surges

0 of 5

Rate 0.0 on the deliberately injected Eid-window surges — many distinct accounts, wide amount spread — which exist precisely to be not flagged. A detector that flags everything scores perfect recall and fails here.

4 · Analytics latency p50 / p95

199–412 ms

Full snapshot rebuild — the call behind GET /api/state — at 200 outlets × 3 providers (200 drawers + 600 provider balances). That is a range, not a point estimate: five runs on the same host gave p50 between 199 and 412 ms and p95 between 250 and 660 ms, so a single number would claim more precision than the measurement has. Per outlet: 0.08–0.23 ms over n=200. Measured on the real snapshot path, not simulated with a delay.

5 · Alert explanation coverage

1.000

Across 25 alerts, every one carried a reason, its evidence and an uncertainty statement. This is a completeness invariant rather than a quality measurement, so it is the one figure the harness asserts — no alert may reach a user without its reasoning.

Why these are measurable at all

The simulator owns the ground truth for every injected episode. That is a deliberate design choice, not an incidental one: it is the only reason precision, recall and false-positive rate can be reported as measurements instead of claims. /api/metrics exposes the same computation against the live snapshot.

How to read these honestly — the caveats travel with the numbers

  • Lead time rests on a small, single-shape sample. All five episodes are the same planted Nagad drain at the same outlet across five seeds, so mean = median = min = max. That is one scenario repeated, not five different scenarios, and it is why the harness records n alongside the figure.
  • Perfect precision and recall describe this simulator, not production traffic. They measure whether the detectors find what was deliberately injected — they cannot speak to patterns nobody thought to simulate.
  • One false-positive family is not a false-positive rate. It is 0 of 5 planted surges, so it establishes the control works; it does not establish a production base rate, which needs a far larger and more varied set of legitimate-demand scenarios.
  • Latency is the only machine-dependent figure here. On a quiet host the p50 has been observed anywhere between 199 ms and 412 ms for the same 200-outlet snapshot, depending on what else was running — which is why the card above gives a range. Treat it as an order of magnitude — a few hundred milliseconds for the whole network — not as a benchmark, and re-measure on the deployment target before quoting it there.

Responsible design

Decision support, not surveillance and not enforcement

Privacy

Every balance, transaction, outlet and account identifier is generated by a seeded simulator; no production API, customer record or provider system is contacted. Accounts are pseudonymous hashes seeded within a run, and there is no field for a name, phone number, account number or national ID — so none can be stored even by accident. No credentials are ever requested, stored or transmitted: no PIN, OTP, password or key, and no login at all.

Human-review boundary

Risk signals are advisory. The escalation path runs agent → field officer → area manager → central operations → risk analyst, and every judgement that matters is made by a person. The platform records review outcomes; it never records a conclusion about a person's conduct.

False positives, treated as engineering

  • An expected demand spike is classified as a spike, not as a concern.
  • A balance mismatch is an integrity fault, never evidence about behaviour.
  • Concentration is judged within the outlet's own baseline — a fairness requirement, not a tuning preference.
  • Confidence and sample-size floors protect analyst attention.
  • Language is enforced by a code-level vocabulary lint, not by good intentions.

Uncertainty, always surfaced

Every projection is an interval with a confidence figure, never a bare point estimate. When a feed is conflicting or missing the projection is withdrawn and replaced with a verification notice. A degraded feed must never silently produce a confident conclusion — and that is tested, not just stated. Confidence multipliers: fresh ×1.00, delayed ×0.80, stale ×0.50, conflicting ×0.30, missing ×0.00.

What the prototype intentionally does not do

  • Real interoperability, settlement or conversion between bKash, Nagad, Rocket or any other wallet.
  • Access to production APIs, real customer identities, real balances or real transaction accounts.
  • Automatic blocking, accusation, disciplinary action or any final determination about a person.
  • Unauthorised cash movement, wallet refill, transfer, recovery or reversal.
  • Collection of credentials, PINs, OTPs, passwords or private authentication data.
  • Any claim of regulatory approval or production readiness.

An unusual pattern is not proof of anything. No output of this platform determines wrongdoing, accuses any person, blocks any transaction, or moves any money. The platform is not a risk-scoring engine and not an enforcement mechanism.

Regulatory position, stated plainly

This is a prototype built for a hackathon, using synthetic data only. It has not been reviewed or approved by any regulator and is not connected to any financial infrastructure. Nothing in it should be read as a claim of compliance with any regulatory framework, nor as evidence that the approach is ready for production.

Data and simulation · 20% of the score

Ground truth is planted on purpose — it is the only reason metrics exist

How the world is built

  • 12 outlets across four real areas — Sylhet/Zindabazar, Dhaka/Mirpur, Chattogram/Agrabad, Rajshahi/Boalia — each with coordinates, so distance-based support discovery is a real calculation rather than a label.
  • One shared cash balance per outlet (৳45,000–160,000) and three independent provider balances — bKash, Nagad, Rocket — opening between ৳40,000 and ৳190,000, with separate feed timestamps and separate feed health.
  • Six hours of history in 18 steps on an afternoon-weighted demand curve, for the pre-Eid market scenario. 62% of transactions are cash-out — the direction that empties the drawer while growing provider e-money.
  • Identifiers are seeded pseudonyms (h0123), stable within a run so burst and relationship analysis work, and derived from no real person.
  • No future-dated transactions — anything stamped after now is dropped, so the trailing window can never manufacture an upward slope from data that has not happened yet.

Assumptions, stated as assumptions

Demand is diurnal and locally peaked · providers are logically separate systems with no shared ledger and no synchronisation between them · cash-out dominates under pressure because that is the direction that produces a service failure · feed faults are per-provider, not per-outlet, which is what makes the degraded-data fallback meaningful. And thresholds are judgement calls, not constants of nature: all of them live in app/config.py so they can be inspected and challenged.

Limitations and next steps

What this prototype is not, and what we would build next

Validation limits

Ground truth is planted, not observed. Precision and recall measure whether the detector finds what the simulator deliberately injected — a genuine measurement of the detector, but it cannot tell us how it behaves against patterns nobody thought to simulate, least of all patterns designed to evade these specific rules.

One control case

The false-positive check rests on a single legitimate-demand control. Salary-day and market-day contexts are handled, but a production system would need many more legitimate scenarios before a false-positive rate could be quoted with any confidence.

Not calibrated to real traffic

Absolute transaction counts and amounts are plausible but derive from no published dataset. The analytics are validated for internal consistency and detection behaviour, not for accuracy against real Bangladeshi agent traffic.

Per-agent baselines miss thin network patterns

Per-outlet baselining is the right fairness call, but a network-wide pattern spread thinly across many individually unremarkable outlets would not be caught. The relationship view that partially covers this is a lead for a human, never a finding.

Deployment and operational limits, stated honestly

Case history is transient. On the free tier the append-only audit log lives on ephemeral disk, so acknowledgements, escalations, notes and resolutions taken during a demo are lost on restart or redeploy. Every generated value is recomputed from the seed on startup, so the dashboard always renders correctly — but the coordination record is not durable. In any real deployment the audit trail is a compliance artifact and would require a managed database with the append-only table replicated off the container; that is a connection string in app/config.py, a deployment decision rather than an architectural one.

Also: single instance, no autoscaling, no failover, no SLA, a ~50 second cold start on the free tier, and a public URL with no authentication — acceptable only because every byte of data is synthetic and there is nothing private to protect. It would not be acceptable with real data. The what-if demand multiplier is first-order: it scales the drain rate and inverts the exhaustion time, but does not model customers switching providers, queueing, or leaving.

Next steps, in the order we would do them

1 · Durable audit

Move the append-only case-event table to a managed database so the coordination record survives a deploy. This is the single biggest gap between the prototype and something an operations team could rely on.

2 · Widen the control set

Add legitimate salary-day, market-day, festival and remittance-window scenarios so the false-positive rate is measured against a realistic base rate rather than one control case — and report it as a rate, with its denominator.

3 · Calibrate against real distributions

Replace plausible-but-invented volumes with real aggregate distributions through a privacy-preserving, provider-side process — never by importing customer-level data into the prototype. Only then does an absolute number mean anything.

4 · Close the feedback loop

Let reviewer outcomes — confirmed, dismissed, inconclusive — flow back into threshold tuning. Right now app/config.py is the only feedback channel, and it is a human editing a file.

5 · Operational hardening

Authentication with role-scoped views, real persistence, more than one instance, and streaming updates instead of snapshot polling at higher outlet counts.

Deliberately not on this list: any step toward executing or automating a financial action. The advisory boundary is the design, not a stage we intend to grow out of.
